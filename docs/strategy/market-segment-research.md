# Receipt-related market segment research

**Prepared:** 2026-07-27

**Scope:** Ireland-first, primary-source desk research only

This note supports segment selection; it is not customer evidence. External facts below
come from Revenue, the Residential Tenancies Board (RTB), the Competition and Consumer
Protection Commission (CCPC), the Data Protection Commission (DPC), insurers, retailers,
and first-party associations. Strategic inferences and unknowns are labelled. No source
reviewed provides evidence that any segment will pay for a new receipt product.

## Cross-cutting facts

- Revenue requires a person running a business to keep records used to calculate tax,
  including expense and purchase receipts, continuously for all transactions. The
  taxpayer remains responsible even when an accountant holds the records, and records
  generally must be retained for six years
  ([Revenue: keeping records](https://www.revenue.ie/en/starting-a-business/starting-a-business/keeping-records.aspx)).
- Revenue already provides a Receipts Tracker in myAccount and ROS. It accepts receipt
  details and images under both `Trade` and `Rental`, and an uploaded image can replace
  retention of the original
  ([Revenue: Receipts Tracker](https://www.revenue.ie/en/online-services/services/myaccount/manage-your-receipts-with-receipts-tracker.aspx)).
- If an experiment processes personal data, the DPC says it must observe purpose
  limitation, data minimisation, storage limitation, integrity, and confidentiality
  ([DPC: principles of data protection](https://dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/data-protection-basics/principles-data-protection)).

**Strategic inference:** A new product cannot win merely by digitising receipts. It must
demonstrate a downstream outcome that Revenue's tracker, retailer accounts, insurer
records, an accountant, or ordinary file storage does not already provide.

## 1. Self-administering Irish sole traders with recurring business purchases

### Verified external facts

- A sole trader is responsible for business records and submits an annual Form 11.
  Registration as self-employed is required where net income is above €5,000
  ([Revenue: what is a business?](https://www.revenue.ie/en/starting-a-business/what-is-a-business/index.aspx)).
- Deductible costs can include goods for resale, premises costs, vehicle or machine
  running costs, accountancy fees, and qualifying finance costs. Mixed business/private
  expenditure must be apportioned
  ([Revenue: claiming a deduction for expenses](https://www.revenue.ie/en/starting-a-business/claiming-a-deduction-for-expenses/index.aspx)).
- The existing official substitute is Revenue's Receipts Tracker, including its `Trade`
  category. An accountant or tax agent does not transfer the taxpayer's responsibility
  for the underlying records
  ([Revenue: Receipts Tracker](https://www.revenue.ie/en/online-services/services/myaccount/manage-your-receipts-with-receipts-tracker.aspx);
  [Revenue: keeping records](https://www.revenue.ie/en/starting-a-business/starting-a-business/keeping-records.aspx)).
- LEOs provide advisory services to people exploring self-employment and existing
  businesses, and operate training and networking events through 31 local offices
  ([LEO: training programmes](https://www.localenterprise.ie/discover-business-supports/training-programmes/)).
  ISME also operates an Irish SME owner-manager membership and events network
  ([ISME membership](https://isme.ie/about-isme-membership/)).

### Strategic assessment

- **Trigger and problem:** Every business purchase creates a record; the salient moments
  are regular bookkeeping and the annual tax return. The hypothesised pain is converting
  scattered evidence into an expense record with the correct business purpose and
  business/private split.
- **Frequency and consequence:** The duty is continuous and tax-linked. That verifies
  frequency and consequence, but not that sole traders find the work severe.
- **Workarounds:** ROS, an accountant, retailer invoices, bank statements, folders, and
  bookkeeping products are substitutes. Only ROS is verified here; actual workaround use
  and dissatisfaction remain interview questions.
- **Trust:** Receipts and expense annotations may reveal financial and commercial
  activity. A human-assisted test should request the minimum fields and make purpose,
  access, retention, and deletion explicit.
- **Payer and willingness to pay:** The likely payer is the sole trader. Willingness to
  pay, avoided time, missed deductions, and current software spend are unknown.
- **Access and acquisition:** LEO and ISME are credible gatekeepers, but neither is an
  open participant list. Founder access therefore depends on permission, event
  participation, and direct networks. Interview difficulty is medium.
- **Fast rejection evidence:** Reject this segment if, among ten qualified interviews,
  fewer than four can describe a recent recurring cleanup, missing-record, or
  classification problem and fewer than three will submit representative data for a
  manual fulfilment test. Also reject if the qualified majority is already satisfied
  with ROS or an accountant and cannot name a downstream outcome they lack.

**Strategic inference:** Start with the behavioural niche “Irish sole traders who
personally prepare expense records for their own return or accountant, make recurring
business purchases, and do not already use an integrated bookkeeping workflow,” not all
self-employed workers.

## 2. Self-managing Irish private landlords with one or two properties

### Verified external facts

- The RTB's nationally representative 2022 telephone survey of 500 small landlords found
  that 81% owned one property and 94% described landlording as part-time. A letting
  agent was used for 22% of the surveyed properties
  ([RTB Small Landlord Survey 2023, pp. 5–11 and 24](https://rtb.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/RTB-Small-Landlord-Survey-2023-Report-13.12.23.V1.pdf)).
- In that survey, 75% had received a maintenance request from their current tenant. An
  inventory had been completed when the current tenant moved in for 83% of properties
  ([RTB Small Landlord Survey 2023, pp. 25–29](https://rtb.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/RTB-Small-Landlord-Survey-2023-Report-13.12.23.V1.pdf)).
- Revenue allows specified maintenance, repair, insurance, service, RTB-registration,
  and furniture/white-goods costs, and requires full and accurate expense records for
  each property
  ([Revenue: rental expenses](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/rental-income/irish-rental-income/what-expenses-are-allowed.aspx)).
- Landlords must register a new tenancy and renew registration annually
  ([RTB: register a tenancy](https://rtb.ie/register-tenancies/register-a-tenancy/)).
- Revenue's existing Receipts Tracker has a `Rental` category. The Irish Property Owners
  Association (IPOA) represents landlords from single-unit to portfolio owners and runs
  regional meetings, briefings, training, and networking
  ([Revenue: Receipts Tracker](https://www.revenue.ie/en/online-services/services/myaccount/manage-your-receipts-with-receipts-tracker.aspx);
  [IPOA](https://ipoa.ie/)).

### Strategic assessment

- **Trigger and problem:** Maintenance requests, repairs, replacement appliances,
  tenancy inventories, registration, and the tax return create concrete property-linked
  record events. The hypothesised job is producing a per-property evidence pack rather
  than extracting generic receipt fields.
- **Frequency and consequence:** The RTB survey verifies that maintenance requests are
  common and that most small landlords operate part-time without an agent. Allowable
  expenses affect taxable rental profit, while inventories can matter in a tenancy
  dispute. The time cost and frequency of lost deductions are unknown.
- **Workarounds:** ROS Rental receipts, agents, accountants, property folders, and
  spreadsheets are substitutes. Their adequacy for linking purchase, property,
  maintenance event, asset, and inventory is unverified.
- **Trust:** Property records may include addresses, tenant communications, and
  financial evidence. External testing needs strict minimisation, redaction guidance,
  named reviewer access, and deletion commitments.
- **Payer and willingness to pay:** The likely payer is the landlord. No source supports
  a price or payment propensity.
- **Access and acquisition:** IPOA offers a concentrated gatekeeper and in-person
  settings, but access needs permission. Public RTB data is not a recruitment list.
  Interview and acquisition difficulty are medium.
- **Fast rejection evidence:** Reject if fewer than four of ten self-managing landlords
  report a recent per-property record problem or repeated manual reconciliation, or if
  fewer than three will provide redacted representative records for manual fulfilment.
  Reject if ROS/accountant handoff already produces the desired property evidence pack
  with negligible founder-relevant pain.

**Strategic inference:** This is the strongest fallback and may overtake sole traders if
founder access through IPOA is easier than LEO/ISME access or if early interviews reveal
recurring property-level reconciliation pain.

## 3. Recent buyers managing higher-value warranties and returns

### Verified external facts

- A retailer may require proof of purchase, but CCPC says a receipt is not essential:
  card statements, delivery dockets, or other documentation can suffice. Irish
  faulty-goods remedies can remain available for up to six years
  ([CCPC: faulty-goods FAQs](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/faulty-products-and-goods/faulty-goods-faqs)).
- In 2025, 42,791 consumers contacted the CCPC helpline and reported average spend of
  €6,292 on the disputed product or service. One fifth of contacts concerned faulty
  goods or services, and 61% of followed-up consumers had contacted the trader five or
  more times. These figures establish consequential consumer problems, but do not
  isolate high-value goods or missing receipts
  ([CCPC 2025 helpline report](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2026/04/13/ccpc-consumer-helpline-report-2025--online-purchases--home-improvement-issues-and-cars)).
- Retailer/manufacturer systems are substitutes. Currys accepts a receipt, bank
  statement, or online invoice; Apple provides online-order invoices and may require
  original proof to correct a device purchase date
  ([Currys returns](https://www.currys.ie/services/shopping-with-us/returns-refunds.html);
  [Apple returns and invoices](https://www.apple.com/ie/shop/help/returns_refund);
  [Apple proof of purchase](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102261)).

### Strategic assessment

- **Trigger/severity:** A fault, recall, return deadline, or warranty claim is salient
  and can have a large monetary consequence, but is episodic.
- **Workaround weakness:** Evidence is fragmented across email, retailer accounts, bank
  statements, and paper. Conversely, those alternatives mean lost receipts do not
  necessarily block redress.
- **Trust/payer:** Purchase histories are sensitive; the individual consumer is the
  likely payer, but ongoing payment for an episodic event is especially uncertain.
- **Access:** The segment is hard to identify before the trigger. CAI or retailer
  partnerships are possible gatekeepers, not immediate founder-owned channels.
- **Fast rejection evidence:** Defer or reject if fewer than three of ten people with a
  recent high-value return, repair, or claim say locating proof delayed or materially
  weakened the outcome. Positive opinions from people without a recent trigger do not
  count.

## 4. Insured households preparing contents inventories

### Verified external facts

- AIG advises walking through every room to make an inventory when setting contents
  cover, reviewing sums insured at least annually, retaining photographic evidence for
  high-value items, and keeping valuations current for specified items
  ([AIG contents guidance](https://www.aig.ie/home/solutions/home-insurance/contents-insurance);
  [AIG home FAQs](https://www.aig.ie/home/solutions/home-insurance/home-insurance-faqs)).
- Allianz asks claimants for loss details and says photos or receipts can be uploaded,
  but a claim can proceed without them; theft claims may prompt requests for receipts,
  manuals, or valuations
  ([Allianz home claims](https://www.allianz.ie/claims/home-insurance.html)).
- Brokers Ireland provides a searchable directory of home-insurance brokers. That is a
  reachable intermediary channel, not a directory of qualified households
  ([Brokers Ireland: find a broker](https://brokersireland.ie/broker/)).

### Strategic assessment

- **Trigger/frequency:** Quotes, renewals, moving home, and acquiring a high-value item
  are planned triggers; theft, fire, and flood are urgent triggers. Annual review is
  recommended, but actual household behaviour is unknown.
- **Consequence and substitute:** Underinsurance or weak claim evidence can be costly.
  The present official workflow is a room-by-room list plus photos, and receipts are not
  always necessary.
- **Trust/payer/access:** A full inventory exposes detailed household possessions and
  potentially the home address. The household is the likely payer, but insurers or
  brokers may be the more plausible channel. Direct acquisition is difficult.
- **Fast rejection evidence:** Reject if a broker cannot identify a meaningful evidence
  gap, or if fewer than three of ten renewing homeowners complete even a founder-led
  inventory and use it to change cover or document a claim.

## 5. Amateur photographers tracking valuable equipment

### Verified external facts

- Aviva names cameras, binoculars, bicycles, and sports equipment as items that may need
  specified cover. AIG says high-value portable items may need specified cover and asks
  for identifying descriptions or serial numbers; items above €5,000 require valuations
  under its stated process
  ([Aviva optional cover](https://www.aviva.ie/insurance/home-insurance/optional-cover/);
  [AIG home FAQs](https://www.aig.ie/home/solutions/home-insurance/home-insurance-faqs)).
- The Irish Photographic Federation is the national body for camera clubs and publishes
  a geographically distributed list of affiliated clubs
  ([Irish Photographic Federation](https://irishphoto.ie/);
  [affiliated clubs](https://irishphoto.ie/clubs/)).

### Strategic assessment

- **Narrowing:** “Collectors and hobbyists” is not one segment. Amateur photographers
  with multiple insured bodies/lenses are a testable niche with a first-party club
  channel.
- **Trigger/problem:** Purchase, resale, insurance renewal, travel, theft, or damage can
  require model, serial, value, proof, and photographs. The hypothesised problem is
  maintaining a current evidence pack as equipment changes.
- **Frequency/consequence:** Item loss can be costly, but purchase cadence, inventory
  churn, claim frequency, and current record failure are unknown.
- **Trust/payer/access:** Publishing a valuable-equipment list could increase perceived
  security risk. The owner is the likely payer. Club organisers make interviews
  comparatively accessible, subject to permission.
- **Fast rejection evidence:** Reject if fewer than four of ten qualified photographers
  maintain at least five items needing identification/value records, or fewer than three
  will build and later reuse a founder-assisted equipment pack.

## Research recommendation

Begin interviews with **Irish sole traders who personally prepare expense records, make
recurring business purchases, and lack an integrated bookkeeping workflow**. The record
duty is continuous, the payer and user are the same person, and LEO/ISME provide
plausible permissioned access. Treat the free Revenue tracker as the main falsification
pressure.

Use **self-managing landlords with one or two properties** as the fallback. This segment
has the strongest segment-specific external evidence of recurring operational triggers,
but property/tenant trust risk is higher and founder access depends more heavily on an
association gatekeeper.

Defer warranties/returns and household inventories because their triggers are episodic
and substitutes are strong. Do not test “collectors/hobbyists” broadly; retain amateur
photographers as a narrow later test because clubs make them unusually reachable.

## Unknowns requiring interviews or founder judgment

- Which segment the founder can reach through warm contacts or permissioned gatekeepers.
- Current workaround, active minutes per month/year, errors, lost deductions or failed
  claims, and whether the receipt is actually the bottleneck.
- Whether representative or real documents can be handled without unacceptable privacy
  risk, and whether participants accept human review.
- The downstream outcome valuable enough to cause repeat use.
- Any credible commitment to pay. No desk-research fact in this note supplies one.
